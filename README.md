# ART/WORK
## A shared understanding, not a contract
This isn't a market with an organiser. It's a truck, parked in the same place, on the same rough schedule, because people keep showing up when it is. Nobody runs this. Everyone taking part is responsible for their own side of it. This page just makes sure everyone's starting from the same understanding.

## No membership. No registration.
There's no list, no sign-up, no fee to take part. You turn up, or you don't. Nobody is vetted, admitted, or excluded by anyone running the thing — because nobody is running it in that sense.

## Every exchange is between the two people making it.
Whatever you trade, swap, or promise is between you and the other person. Nobody else sets the terms, prices it, or guarantees it. If you're not happy with an exchange, that's between you and the person you made it with — not a dispute for anyone else to settle.

## You are responsible for your own barters — legally and otherwise.
Barter (permuta) between private individuals is a lawful contract. Occasional exchange between individuals generally needs no registration or declaration. If your own trading becomes regular enough, or produces enough of a financial gain, to count as a proper income-producing activity, that's your responsibility to understand and, if needed, declare — the same as it would be anywhere else, whether or not any of it involves money. Ask a commercialista if you're ever unsure where that line sits for you.

## A promise is a promise between two people, held by those who know you.
If you promise something instead of trading an object, that promise is between you and the person you made it to. It's chalked up on the board so everyone can see it — but the board doesn't enforce anything, and neither does anyone else present. What holds it in place is your word, and the people who know you well enough to notice if you don't keep it.

## The truck provides a time and a place. Nothing else.
Whoever brings the truck is not managing a market, taking a cut, approving what's offered, or vouching for anyone's exchange. They're demonstrating how this works, for anyone who hasn't seen it before. That's all the role is.

## No signs. No branding. You represent yourself.
There's no name painted on the truck, no logo, no association to join. A sign or a brand exists to reassure strangers who have no other way to trust each other — that's not what this is. You take part by simply being present, in person, in public. That already says more than any sign could. This is not a clandestine market hiding from anyone. It's the opposite: everything happens openly, in the square, in front of whoever wants to watch.

## Use common sense.
Nothing illegal, nothing dangerous, nothing you don't actually have the right to offer. Beyond that — no rules, only trust.

No gatekeepers. No authority. Just people, trusting each other, in public.
